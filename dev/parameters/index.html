Parameters

A simulation is described by a Params value. Its defaults are QTSim's (SetDefOptions in reference/QTSim/src/PassOptionFile.c), and checkparams checks the values when a World is made. Copy one with changes with Params(p; field = value). read_qtsim_options and write_qtsim_options translate QTSim's options files.

What the parameters set, and what they don't

PhenotypeVar and Heritability set the environmental variance VE = (1 − h²)·VP, the mutation variance and the analytical width ω. They do not set the realised variances. The genetic variance starts at zero (every allelic effect is 0) and settles where mutation and selection balance. That level depends on m, ω, the number of loci and N, and can be well above or below h²·VP. So the realised VP and h² of a run are outputs, not inputs.

Each generation the optimum θ moves, from MoveFitnessSDGen on, by MoveType:

  • :phenotype: θ = z̄ + √V_P·d, a fixed lead over the current mean phenotype;
  • :fitness: θ = z̄ + ω·d, the same in units of the fitness width;
  • :continuous: θ = θ + d, a constant rate of environmental change; the paper's published runs use this.

Two more moves, which QTSim lacks, make the optimum fluctuate around a linear trend of d per generation, as field environments do. With t₀ = MoveFitnessSDGen and θ₀ the optimum before the move (0, unless something else has set it), from generation t₀ on:

  • :red_noise: θt = θ₀ + d (t − t₀) + εt, where ε is a stationary AR(1) process with standard deviation σ = MoveNoiseSD and autocorrelation ρ = MoveAutocorr: ε{t₀} ~ N(0, σ²), then εt = ρ ε{t−1} + σ √(1 − ρ²) ξt with ξ_t ~ N(0, 1). σ sets the size of the fluctuations and ρ their time scale, about −1/ln ρ generations, without changing their variance. ρ = 0 is white noise. The move takes one normal draw per generation from the run's random numbers, so it changes the rest of the run's draws.
  • :periodic: θ_t = θ₀ + d (t − t₀) + A sin(2π (t − t₀)/P), with A = MoveAmplitude and P = MovePeriod. It draws nothing.

Both are exogenous: θ does not depend on the population. d may be 0. The trend starts at θ₀ in generation t₀, one step behind :continuous, which has already added d by then. In a metapopulation each deme has its own noise. write_qtsim_options refuses these moves. The SLiM model that slim_script generates has both (moveType, moveNoiseSD, moveAutocorr, moveAmplitude, movePeriod), and agrees with the kernel (validate/slim/compare_fluctuating.jl). The moments lowering (run_moments) follows a periodic optimum exactly and red noise's trend, or a given sequence of optima. The steady lag and load are periodic_response, red_noise_lag_var, expected_lag2 and lag_load.

Fitness is QTSim's Gaussian pdf(Normal(0, ω), z − θ) / (0.4/ω), which keeps QTSim's 0.4 in place of 1/√(2π), so the fitness at the optimum is 0.997 rather than 1. Selection is viability selection: an individual survives if a uniform draw falls below its fitness. The population dies when fewer than two survive (one, if haploid).

Population size

QTSim keeps the population at PopSize: however few survive selection, they produce PopSize offspring. A population there dies only when fewer than two survive a single generation, and it pays nothing for a low mean fitness until then.

With Demography = :beverton_holt the survivors' offspring are density-regulated. With n survivors, carrying capacity K = PopSize and R = GrowthRate, the next generation has Poisson(R n/(1 + (R − 1) n/K)) individuals, at most K (noffspring). A population with mean fitness W̄ settles near K (R W̄ − 1)/((R − 1) W̄) and shrinks when R W̄ < 1, so it can decline and be rescued by adaptation, or die. The SLiM model that slim_script generates draws the number of offspring the same way, and the two backends agree (validate/slim/compare_demography.jl). A population that leaves no offspring dies in that generation, which, as at selection, has no output row.

QTSim's selection is on viability: an individual survives iff U < W(z), and the survivors mate at random. With Selection = :fecundity everyone survives and each parent is drawn with probability W/ΣW (two distinct parents when diploid), and under :beverton_holt the offspring number is the formula above with n = ΣW = N W̄, the expected number of survivors under viability. The two modes then have the same expected selection; viability adds the variance of who survives. At a fixed size fecundity selection is soft: the population dies only when fewer than Ploidy individuals have positive fitness, which in practice means that fitness underflows to zero. The nsurv column is then N, and sel_diff the differential of the mating weights, Σ W z/ΣW − z̄. The SLiM model draws parents with the same weights (validate/slim/compare_selection.jl).

Parameters of a deme, and of a step

A metapopulation can give each deme its own Params: [p1, p2, p3] ⊗ demes(3) in the kernel, run_moments([p1, p2, p3]; cycle) in the moments lowering and slim_script(cycle, [p1, p2, p3]) in SLiM. The demes run for the same NoGenerations, and migration needs the same NoQTL and Ploidy. In the SLiM model a constant whose value differs between demes holds one value per deme, which each deme keeps on its subpopulation; NoQTL, NoGenerations, Ploidy, CrossoverModel and Demography must be the same in every deme there.

A step of the life cycle can also carry parameters, which override the deme's for that step only (withparams): withparams(L.select; ω = 0.3) selects with width 0.3 whatever the deme's width is, withparams(g; θ = 0.1) is a generation whose selection is toward a fixed optimum 0.1, and lifecycle().migrate(k; rate) migrates at its own rate. The fields a step can override are those its image reads: the move, ω, θ, StandardisePhenotype, Drift and Selection for select; RecombPoissonMean, CrossoverModel, Demography and GrowthRate for reproduce; Sample for record; ChangeFitnessGen and ChangeFitnessSD for rescale; MutationScalar and MutationRatePerGamete for mutate. A step with the deme's own values is the plain step, bit for bit, and a step's fields of Params give the same run as Params with those values. A step's ω and θ leave the deme's state alone, so the omega and theta columns record the deme's.
