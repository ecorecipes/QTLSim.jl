QTLSim.QTLSim — Module
QTLSim

A Julia port of QTSim, the quantitative-genetics simulation of Pagel, Gardner and Meade (Scientific Reports, 2025; the C source calls itself QTLSim 1.0): an additive polygenic trait under Gaussian stabilising viability selection toward an optimum that can move, with random mating, Poisson recombination and Poisson–Gaussian mutation. The kernel reproduces QTSim's numerics; validate/ compares it with QTSim and with the paper's published results.

The categorical layer, in which the population is an ACSet and the life cycle is a composite of operators (⋅) that can be stratified into demes (⊗), is the package extension QTLSimCatlabExt. Julia loads it when Catlab is loaded (import Catlab). Its functions are declared, documented and exported here.

QTLSim.Params — Type
Params

Configuration for one simulation, with QTSim's defaults. Heritability is the narrow-sense heritability h² (a single scalar); MutationScalar is the mutation scalar m. The documentation's Parameters page lists every field with its meaning and its QTSim option. Values are checked when a World is made (see checkparams).

Some fields have no QTSim counterpart, or behave differently:

  • CrossoverModel: QTSim draws Poisson(RecombPoissonMean) crossover points per gamete but only the lowest one switches strands (its point index never advances), so a gamete has at most one crossover. :qtsim (default) reproduces that, as the published simulations did; :all applies every drawn point (the evidently intended Poisson-crossover model). With NoQTL == 1 the two are identical.
  • Drift: QTSim has the option but switches it off at the start of every run, so it never has an effect there. Here Drift = true makes every fitness 1: a neutral run, for comparison.
  • Demography and GrowthRate: QTSim always refills the population to PopSize, so it dies only when fewer than two survive one generation (:fixed, the default). With :beverton_holt the population is density-regulated, with carrying capacity PopSize and GrowthRate offspring per survivor at low density (see noffspring), so that it shrinks when mean fitness is low and can be rescued by adaptation.
  • Selection: QTSim's selection is on viability (:viability, the default): an individual survives iff U < W(z), and survivors mate at random. With :fecundity everyone survives and each parent is drawn with probability W/ΣW (two distinct parents when diploid), and under :beverton_holt the offspring number uses the sum of fitnesses ΣW = N·W̄ in place of the number of survivors (see noffspring). ΣW is the expected number of survivors under viability, so the two have the same expected selection and, at a fixed size, differ only in its variance. At a fixed size fecundity selection is soft: the population dies only if fewer than Ploidy individuals have positive fitness.
  • MoveType = :red_noise or :periodic, with MoveNoiseSD, MoveAutocorr, MoveAmplitude and MovePeriod: QTSim's optimum moves by a rule; these two make it fluctuate, around a linear trend of MoveFitnessSD per generation, with stationary AR(1) noise or a sine wave (see moveoptimum!).
  • NoTraits, MutCorr, SelCorr, MutCov, SelCov and MoveDirection: QTSim has one trait. With NoTraits = n > 1 each locus has a vector of n effects (pleiotropy), a mutation adds a draw from N(0, M/(Ploidy·NoQTL·MutationRatePerGamete)), so that M (mutational_covariance) is injected per individual per generation, each trait has environmental variance V_E, independently, and fitness is W(z) = c·exp(−½ (z−θ)ᵀ Ω⁻¹ (z−θ)) with QTSim's c = 1/(0.4√(2π)), so that one trait is QTSim's fitness. Ω = ω² S, where ω is the width (InitFitnessSD or the analytical width, changed by ChangeFitnessSD) and the shape S has unit diagonal and correlation SelCorr, or is SelCov/ω² initially. The optimum moves along the unit vector u ∝ MoveDirection (move_direction): by d·u per generation (:continuous), to z̄ + √V_P·d·u (:phenotype) or to z̄ + ω d u (:fitness). The fluctuating moves have the trend d·u per generation and fluctuate along the unit vector f ∝ FluctDirection (u by default, fluct_direction): A sin(2π t/P)·f (:periodic), or AR(1) noise with stationary covariance MoveNoiseCov, by default MoveNoiseSD²·f fᵀ (move_noise_covariance). FluctDirection and MoveNoiseCov are Julia-only, for several traits. See the design note traits_design.md.
  • Plasticity, OptimumSlope, CueLag and PlasticityCostSD (Julia-only; off with Plasticity = 0, the default): phenotypic plasticity, a linear reaction norm z = g + b·ε + e with slope b = Plasticity, after Chevin, Lande and Mace (2010). The environment ε is measured by the optimum it sets, θ = B·ε with B = OptimumSlope, so the plastic part of the phenotype is (b/B)·θ: a fraction b/B of the optimum's displacement from the origin of the trait's scale is tracked without any genetic change. The cue is the environment CueLag = τ generations before selection (τ = 0: a perfect cue; the environment before the run is taken to be the first one seen), so z_t = g + (b/B)·θ_{t−τ} + e. A cost of plasticity multiplies every fitness by exp(−b²/(2ω_b²)), ω_b = PlasticityCostSD (Inf: no cost): stabilising selection on the slope around 0. The reaction norm is expressed in select, once the optimum of the generation is set (select!). It needs an optimum with its own trajectory: :continuous, :red_noise, :periodic, or none; QTSim's :phenotype and :fitness moves re-anchor the optimum to the mean phenotype every generation, which a plastic shift would only carry along, and are rejected.
  • ReactionNorm = true with NoTraits = 2 (Julia-only): a heritable reaction norm. Trait 1 is the elevation a and trait 2 the slope: an individual's slope is Plasticity + g₂, its phenotype z = g₁ + (Plasticity + g₂)·ε + e with ε = θ_{t−τ}/B, selection acts on z (one-dimensional, width ω) and the cost on the individual's slope. MutCov gives the mutational covariance of elevation and slope. See plasticity_design.md.

QTLSim.checkparams — Function
checkparams(p)

Throw an ArgumentError if p is not a model QTSim would run, or would run differently: Ploidy 1 or 2, at least one individual (two if diploid), locus, sampled generation and mutation per gamete; 0 ≤ Heritability ≤ 1 and PhenotypeVar > 0; MoveType one of :phenotype, :fitness, :continuous, :red_noise, :periodic (QTSim falls back to the phenotype move on an unknown name; a misspelt symbol here would silently change the model); MoveNoiseSD ≥ 0, 0 ≤ MoveAutocorr < 1, a finite MoveAmplitude and MovePeriod > 0; CrossoverModel :qtsim or :all; Selection :viability or :fecundity; the schedules MoveFitnessSDGen and ChangeFitnessGen either −1 (off) or a generation, with ChangeFitnessSD > 0 when the width changes; and InitFitnessSD either −1 (the analytical width, which needs V_E > 0 and m > 0) or positive. Called by World(p).

QTLSim.World — Type
World(p; blocksize = default_blocksize(p.NoQTL), pool = nothing)

Mutable population state carried through the operators. Holds the current parents' genomes and environments, per-individual scratch (genotype, phenotype, fitness), the survivor index set, the current selection optimum θ and width ω, and a double buffer (schr/senv) for the offspring generation. Under Selection = :fecundity, select! sets weighted and leaves the survivors' mating weights, their fitnesses, as cumulative sums in cumw with total wsum, for reproduce! and noffspring.

The current population is individuals 1:N, with N == PopSize always under QTSim's Demography = :fixed; under density regulation N varies, PopSize is the carrying capacity, and Noff is the number of offspring in the buffer. θ0 and ε hold the state of a fluctuating optimum (MoveType = :red_noise or :periodic, see moveoptimum!).

Genomes

Each chromosome of NoQTL loci is split into nb blocks of blocksize loci (the last may be shorter), ⌈√NoQTL⌉ by default, and stored as nb ids of blocks in a pool (QTLSim.BlockPool): w.chr[k, c, i] is block k of chromosome c of individual i, an (nb, Ploidy, PopSize) array, and w.schr the same for the offspring. Blocks are shared and reference-counted. A gamete shares every block of its parent's chromosomes except the one that holds a crossover, which it builds; a mutation changes a block in place if nothing else holds it and copies it first otherwise. Each block caches the sum of its values, and the genotype of individual i (QTSim CaclGenotype) is the sum of its blocks' sums, chromosome by chromosome, block by block. So a generation costs about N·√L operations rather than N·L.

Read the genomes with genomes and write them with setgenomes!, which convert to and from a dense (NoQTL, Ploidy, n) array.

Chromosomes that hold the same block are identical by descent over it (shared_blocks); anc is nothing unless a pedigree is being recorded (record_pedigree!).

Every slot of both buffers, all PopSize columns, holds one reference to a live block at all times. A slot drops its reference only when it is overwritten, after it has taken the new one: by reproduce! in the offspring buffer, which after a swap holds the previous parents, by mutate! when it copies a block, by setgenomes!, and by migration between demes that do not share a pool. So the previous parents' blocks are released one offspring slot at a time during the next reproduce!, never while a slot names them, and swapbuffers! moves no references. Demes of a metapopulation made by ⊗ share one pool, so that a migrant is moved by swapping its block ids.

The random draws, and so the allele values, are those of a dense array of alleles (bit for bit, for the same seed). Genotypes are summed in another order, so they can differ from a sum over the dense array in the last bits (about 1e-16 relative).

QTLSim.simulate — Function
simulate(p::Params; seed=p.Seed, init=:zero, burnin=nothing, at=QTLSim.first_event(p), target=:genic, spectrum=nothing)

Run one simulation from Params with a MersenneTwister(seed), returning the recorded statistics (see run!) and the seed. Without a seed one is drawn at random, and the returned seed reproduces the run.

init = :zero (default) starts from genomes of zeros at generation 0, as QTSim does. init = :equilibrium starts from a population drawn from the stationary allele-frequency spectrum, burnin generations (required) before generation at of the protocol (equilibrium_world): generations before at − burnin are not run and not in the output, the rest keep their numbers. The output then also has start, the first generation run. The same generator draws the population and then runs it. target and spectrum are equilibrium_genomes's.

The same seed gives the same run with the same versions of Julia and Distributions.jl: the streams of rand(rng, 1:n) and of the Poisson sampler are not guaranteed across their releases.

QTLSim.run! — Function
run!(w, rng; record=true, resume=false)

Iterate the generation operator for NoGenerations, replicating QTSim's RunSim order: develop → select → reproduce → (record) → fitness-width change → mutate → swap. Returns a NamedTuple of per-sampled-generation statistics (gen, pheno_mean, pheno_var, geno_var, nsurv, theta, omega) plus extinct/extinct_gen.

With resume = true the run continues a World that already has its environmental deviations, from its generation w.gen to NoGenerations − 1, without drawing the founders' environments again: for a World from equilibrium_world, which starts at a later generation of the protocol.

QTLSim.record! — Function
record!(rec, w)

Append the parent generation's statistics to the columns rec (see records) every Sample generations (QTSim Output); a no-op when rec is nothing. Variances are sample variances (n − 1), as GSL's. Besides QTSim's columns it records the mean fitness W̄ (one minus the load) and the selection differential, the survivors' mean phenotype minus the mean phenotype, and the population size N (PopSize, unless the population is density-regulated). QTLSim.develop! — Function
develop!(w)

Genotype = Σ allele effects; phenotype = genotype + environment (QTSim CaclPopPhenotype). Deterministic given the environments drawn at birth. The sum of the effects is the sum of the cached sums of the individual's blocks, chromosome by chromosome, block by block (see World).

develop!, select! and mutate! serve both models: they call the one-trait operator (develop_one! etc.) or the operator of several traits (develop_traits! etc., src/traits.jl) by the World. The two models' tables of kernel images (ext/QTLSimCatlabExt/compose.jl) call those directly.

QTLSim.select! — Function
select!(w, rng)

Move the optimum, assign each individual a stabilizing-selection fitness, and draw the survivor set (survive iff rand() < fitness). Mirrors QTSim Fitness

  • SetSurviving. Sets w.extinct if too few survive to reproduce.

Under Selection = :fecundity (Julia-only) everyone survives and draws nothing here: the survivors are all N individuals, and their fitnesses become mating weights (w.weighted, w.cumw, w.wsum), which reproduce! samples parents by. The population dies if fewer than Ploidy individuals have positive fitness (no two distinct parents can be drawn), which at a fixed size happens only when fitness underflows to 0.

Fitness is the normalized Gaussian pdf(Normal(0,ω), (z−θ)/sd) / (0.4/ω), replicating QTSim exactly (the 0.4 ≈ 1/√(2π) constant included); the idealized form exp(−(z−θ)²/2ω²) is what Lean StabilizingSelection.lean proves about.

With phenotypic plasticity (Plasticity = b ≠ 0, Julia-only) the reaction norm is expressed here, once the optimum of the generation is set, since the optimum is the measure of the environment (θ = B·ε): every phenotype gains the plastic part (b/B)·θ_{t−τ}, τ = CueLag (QTLSim.express_plasticity!), so that the recorded phenotypes include it, and the cost of plasticity multiplies every fitness by exp(−b²/(2ω_b²)). With a heritable reaction norm (ReactionNorm) the image is QTLSim.select_norm!.

QTLSim.moveoptimum! — Function
moveoptimum!(w, rng = nothing)

Move the optimum θ if the schedule says so (QTSim SetFitnessMean). From generation t0 = MoveFitnessSDGen on, with d = MoveFitnessSD, z̄ the mean phenotype and t the generation:

  • :phenotype: θ = z̄ + √V_P·d;
  • :fitness: θ = z̄ + ω·d;
  • :continuous: θ += d;
  • :red_noise (Julia-only): θ_t = θ0 + d (t − t0) + ε_t, where θ0 is the optimum before the move and ε is a stationary AR(1) process with standard deviation σ = MoveNoiseSD and autocorrelation ρ = MoveAutocorr: ε_{t0} ~ N(0, σ²) and ε_t = ρ ε_{t−1} + σ √(1 − ρ²) ξ_t with ξ_t ~ N(0, 1). One normal draw per generation, from rng;
  • :periodic (Julia-only): θ_t = θ0 + d (t − t0) + A sin(2π (t − t0)/P), with A = MoveAmplitude and P = MovePeriod. Draws nothing.

The two fluctuating moves are exogenous: θ does not depend on the population. Their trend is d per generation, starting from θ0 at t0 (so a step behind :continuous, which already adds d at t0). With ρ = 0 the noise is white; as ρ → 1 it wanders more slowly with the same variance, so σ and ρ set the size and the time scale (−1/ln ρ generations) of the fluctuations separately.

QTLSim.fluctuating_optimum — Function
fluctuating_optimum(p, θ0, t, ε = 0.0)

The optimum of a fluctuating move t generations after it starts, from θ0: θ0 + d t + ε for MoveType = :red_noise, with the current noise ε, and θ0 + d t + A sin(2π t/P) for :periodic (see moveoptimum!). With ε = 0 it is the expected optimum under red noise.

QTLSim.reproduce! — Function
reproduce!(w, rng)

Fill the offspring buffer by fitness-independent random mating among survivors (QTSim NewIn). Haploid: clone a random survivor. Diploid: two distinct survivors each contribute a (recombined) gamete, drawn as by gamete!. After fecundity selection (w.weighted, see select!) each parent is drawn with probability proportional to its fitness instead, and a second parent equal to the first is redrawn in the same way. Offspring get a fresh environment. How many offspring is noffspring's: PopSize, as in QTSim, or under density regulation a number that depends on the survivors.

Offspring share their parents' blocks (see World); each slot it fills releases the block it named before, from the generation before the parents.

QTLSim.noffspring — Function
noffspring(w, rng)

The number of offspring the survivors produce. Under QTSim's Demography = :fixed it is PopSize, whatever the number of survivors, and draws nothing. Under :beverton_holt, with n survivors, carrying capacity K = PopSize and R = GrowthRate offspring per survivor at low density, it is Poisson(R n / (1 + (R − 1) n / K)), at most K: the population stays near K while survivors are plentiful, and declines when R·W̄ < 1.

Under Selection = :fecundity nobody dies at selection, and n is the sum of the fitnesses, ΣW = N·W̄, a real number: the expected number of survivors under viability selection with the same phenotypes. So the expected offspring number is the viability one's with n replaced by its expectation. As n ↦ R n/(1 + (R − 1) n/K) is concave, viability's expectation is lower, by at most R (R − 1) Var(n)/K ≤ R (R − 1) N/(4K) offspring (Var(n) = Σ W(1 − W) ≤ N/4), about 0.5 for R = 2 and N = K: negligible beside the Poisson noise.

QTLSim.mutate! — Function
mutate!(w, rng)

Add Gaussian mutations to the offspring buffer (QTSim MutateIndividualChrNo). Each chromosome receives Poisson(L·rate) mutations, each of variance m/(ploidy·L·rate)·V_E; in expectation this injects m·V_E of variance per individual per generation (Lean mutationalVarianceInput_eq).

A mutation changes its block in place if the offspring is the only holder, and a copy of it otherwise; the block's sum is then recomputed from its values.

QTLSim.gamete! — Function
gamete!(dest, chrA, chrB, rng, recombMean, L, allpoints=false, pts=Int[])

Build one gamete from a parent's two chromosomes (QTSim CreateCombMap + RecombinationPoissonChr). Draw Poisson(recombMean) distinct crossover points in 0:L-2 (none if the count is 0 or ≥ L), start on a random parental chromosome, and switch strands after crossover points. QTSim never advances its point index, so only the LOWEST point switches: a gamete has at most one crossover. That is the default; allpoints=true switches at every point (Params CrossoverModel = :all). With L == 1 both reduce to picking one parental chromosome at random (Mendelian segregation).

This is the gamete on dense arrays of alleles. The kernel (reproduce!) builds gametes from shared blocks, with the same random draws in the same order, so it copies the same alleles.

Populations initialised from theory

A run from zero genomes spends its first thousands of generations building its standing variation. simulate(p; init = :equilibrium, burnin = B) (and replicate with the same keywords) instead starts from a population drawn from the stationary allele-frequency spectrum of mutation, stabilising selection and drift (stationary_spectrum, src/init.jl), B generations before the first change of selection, and keeps the protocol's generation numbers. The draw is in linkage equilibrium; the burn-in restores the rest. How long it must be, and what it saves, is measured in validate/init_equilibrium.jl. One diploid trait, fixed size, viability selection, kernel only.

QTLSim.equilibrium_world — Function
equilibrium_world(p, rng; burnin, at = QTLSim.first_event(p), target = :genic, mean = :stationary,
                  spectrum = stationary_spectrum(p))

A World started near its mutation–selection–drift equilibrium instead of from zero genomes: genomes from equilibrium_genomes, environmental deviations drawn (init_env!), and the generation counter at g0 = max(0, at − burnin) (QTLSim.protocol_start). Continue it with run!(w, rng; resume = true), which runs generations g0:NoGenerations−1 of the protocol: the first burnin of them, before generation at (by default the generation of the first change of selection, the move of the optimum in the published protocol), are the burn-in that restores what the sampler leaves out, and all keep the protocol's generation numbers, so output rows, the move and the width change are where they are in a run from zero; only generations before g0 are missing. simulate(p; init = :equilibrium, burnin) does both.

The optimum starts at 0 and the width at the initial one, as in World(p). Supported: one diploid trait, fixed size, viability selection (see equilibrium_genomes). How much burn-in is needed depends on the regime: validate/init_equilibrium.jl.

QTLSim.equilibrium_genomes — Function
equilibrium_genomes(p, rng; target = :genic, mean = :stationary, spectrum = stationary_spectrum(p)) -> G

Genomes of a population near its mutation–selection–drift equilibrium at the optimum 0: a dense (NoQTL, 2, PopSize) array of allele values (as genomes gives and setgenomes! takes), drawn from the stationary allele-frequency spectrum stationary_spectrum(p) (src/init.jl): a Poisson number of segregating sites, each with a number of copies k, a frequency and an effect from the stationary density (neutral proposals thinned by QTLSim.sojourn_ratio), at a random locus, carried by k chromosomes drawn at random; an allele is the sum of the effects of the sites its chromosome carries at its locus.

The sites are independent: the population is in Hardy–Weinberg and linkage equilibrium, without the negative linkage disequilibrium that stabilising selection maintains and without the genealogy of the alleles within a locus. A burn-in restores those (see equilibrium_world).

  • target: the expected genic variance. :genic (default) scales the effects so that it is multilocus_equilibrium(p; drift = true).genic, the genic variance of the moments lowering's equilibrium, above its V_G by the linkage disequilibrium that the burn-in will build; :spectrum leaves the spectrum's own variance, stationary_spectrum(p).VG; a number is used as it is (for example the genic variance measured in a few populations burned in from zero).
  • mean: :stationary (default) shifts all alleles so that the mean genotype is a draw from its stationary law around the optimum, N(0, S/(n(2 − V_G/S))) (QTLSim.stationary_mean_var); a number puts the mean genotype there.

spectrum takes a stationary_spectrum(p) computed before, and a numeric target avoids solving the moments equilibrium, for many draws of one p (as replicate does).

One diploid trait, fixed size, viability selection, no StandardisePhenotype, and a fixed reaction norm (Plasticity) only without a cost (PlasticityCostSD = Inf); anything else is an ArgumentError.

QTLSim.stationary_spectrum — Function
stationary_spectrum(p) -> (; VG, nparents, S, Wbar, gene, α2, theta, L, chromosomes)

The stationary state of the allele-frequency spectrum of segregating mutations under mutation, stabilising selection at a fixed optimum and drift, in closed form (src/init.jl): the model of spectrum_equilibrium in continuous frequencies and effects, which equilibrium_genomes samples. The density of sites of effect a at frequency x is

φ(a, x) = 4nLμ g(a) e^{−γ x(1−x)} F_γ(x)/(x(1 − x)),   γ = 2n(a² + 2c)/S,

with g the density of N(0, α²), F_γ(x) = ∫ₓ¹e^{γy(1−y)}dy/∫₀¹e^{γy(1−y)}dy, n = N·W̄ parents, S = ω² + V_G + V_E and c = V_G/(2L); VG is the root of

V = 4nLμ E_a[a² J(γ(a))],   J(γ) = ∫₀¹ e^{−γ x(1−x)} dx

(found by bisection; n, S and γ depend on V). Returns that VG (the expected sample variance of the genotypes of a population drawn from the spectrum, in linkage equilibrium), nparents = n, S, Wbar, gene = c, α2, the neutral scale theta = 4nLμ, the loci L and the chromosomes, 2N; QTLSim.stationary_sites gives the expected number of segregating sites. Its limits are the neutral 2nV_m, the house of cards 4LμS and a Gaussian balance; it is close to house_of_gauss_vg and to spectrum_vg(p). With Drift = true (no selection) it is the neutral spectrum. One diploid trait, fixed size, viability selection.

QTLSim.stationary_mean_var — Function
QTLSim.stationary_mean_var(p, st = stationary_spectrum(p); VG = st.VG) -> Var(z̄ − θ)

The stationary variance of the mean genotype around a fixed optimum: the mean follows z̄′ − θ = (1 − b)(z̄ − θ) + ε with b = V_G/S (the Gaussian response to stabilising selection) and Var ε = V_G/n (drift among n parents), so its variance settles at S/(n(2 − b)), about (ω² + V_P)/(2N_e) (Lande 1976). 0 without selection (Drift = true), where the mean has no stationary law.

QTLSim.sojourn_mass — Function
QTLSim.sojourn_mass(γ) -> J

J(γ) = ∫₀¹ e^{−γ x(1−x)} dx, the factor by which stabilising (underdominant) selection of scaled strength γ = 2n(a² + 2c)/S reduces the variance that the segregating sites of effect a hold, against the neutral spectrum (src/init.jl): J(0) = 1, J ≈ 1 − γ/6 for small γ and J ≈ 2/γ for large. By Gauss–Legendre quadrature, to about 1e-13.

QTLSim.sweep — Function
sweep(f, jobs; cache = nothing, id = identity, cost = nothing,
      ntasks = Threads.nthreads(), progress = 0)

Apply f to every job in jobs and return the results in the order of jobs.

Jobs are handed out one at a time to ntasks tasks on Julia's threads, so a long job never holds up a queue of others (the greedy scheduling of Threads.@threads :greedy, which needs Julia 1.11; :dynamic gives each thread a fixed block of jobs). With cost, a function of a job, the costliest jobs start first, which shortens a sweep whose jobs differ in cost. Neither changes the results, provided f takes its randomness from the job (a seed) rather than from a shared generator: the results then do not depend on the number of threads or on the order in which the jobs run.

With cache, the path of a CSV file, f must return a NamedTuple of numbers, Bools, strings or symbols, with the same fields for every job. Each row is appended to the file as soon as it is computed, after a first column id holding string(id(job)), and jobs whose id is already in the file are not run again, so an interrupted sweep resumes where it stopped. The rows returned are those of the file, for jobs run now as for jobs run before, so a resumed sweep returns the same rows as an uninterrupted one. Each column comes back as the narrowest of Bool, Int, Float64 and String that reads all its values (Float64s round-trip exactly; symbols, and strings that read as numbers, do not). A line cut short by an interruption is dropped. Two jobs with the same id, and a file whose columns differ from the rows, are errors; rows of ids not among jobs are kept in the file and ignored.

If f throws, no further jobs start, the jobs already running finish (and are cached), and the first error is logged with its job and rethrown.

progress = k prints a line every k finished jobs.

# 20 runs of each of three mutation scalars, cached, resumable
jobs = [(; m, seed) for m in (0.001, 0.005, 0.02) for seed in 1:20]
rows = sweep(jobs; cache = "runs.csv", id = j -> "$(j.m)-$(j.seed)") do j
    o = simulate(Params(MutationScalar = j.m, NoGenerations = 2000); seed = j.seed)
    (; j.m, j.seed, VG = o.geno_var[end])
end

QTLSim.read_qtsim_options — Function
read_qtsim_options(path_or_io) -> Params

Read a QTSim options file into Params, as QTSim's PassOptFile reads it:

  • one Keyword value… per line, keywords case-insensitive;
  • a line whose first word starts with # is a comment;
  • MoveFitnessSD <generation> <size> and ChangeFitnessSD <generation> <width> take two values;
  • StandardisePhenotype and Drift take none and switch their option each time they appear.

The seed (Seed) goes into Params' Seed field. Note that QTLSim's random numbers are not QTSim's, so the same seed reproduces a QTLSim run, not the QTSim run.

Where the files differ from Params' field names:

QTSim keywordParams field
GenerationsNoGenerations
MoveFitnessSD g dMoveFitnessSDGen = g, MoveFitnessSD = d
ChangeFitnessSD g wChangeFitnessGen = g, ChangeFitnessSD = w
OptimumMoveTypeMoveType (:phenotype, :fitness, :continuous)

The other keywords (Sample, PopSize, NoQTL, Ploidy, PhenotypeVar, Heritability, MutationScalar, MutationRatePerGamete, InitFitnessSD, StandardisePhenotype) are field names.

Two rules differ from QTSim's:

  • An unknown keyword is an ArgumentError, as it is an error in QTSim. So is an unknown move type, which QTSim silently ignores, keeping the phenotype move.
  • Drift is read but dropped with a warning: QTSim switches drift off at the start of every run, so the option never has an effect there.

QTLSim.gaussian_vg_loci — Function
gaussian_vg_loci(p; drift = false)

The equilibrium genetic variance of the multilocus Gaussian model, the fixed point that the moments lowering converges to with loci = :multilocus (run_moments(p; loci = :multilocus); the default for several traits, QTLSim.moments_loci). For one trait the default is the rare-alleles closure, whose fixed point is rare_alleles_vg. Without drift, the positive root of V²/(V_s + V) = n·V_m,

V = (n V_m + √(n² V_m² + 4 n V_m V_s))/2,

with V_s = selection_variance(p), V_m = muteVar(p) and n = 2L for a diploid of L loci that recombine (n = 2 without recombination, n = 1 for a haploid, whose offspring are clones). Each allele position balances mutation against the part of selection's reduction that its share of the variance inherits, V²/(n s), while the rest is linkage disequilibrium, which recombination breaks up; at equilibrium the genic variance exceeds V by the LD, and V itself does not depend on the rate of recombination (Lande 1975; Bulmer 1980; Turelli and Barton 1990). It is gaussian_vg_exact with n·V_m in place of V_m, and √(n V_m V_s) for weak mutation (Lande's per-locus sum).

With drift = true, the stationary expected within-population sample variance of the same recursion with drift among N = PopSize adults and their parents (survivors, or N W̄²/E[W²] under fecundity selection; multilocus_equilibrium), which is what run_moments(p; loci = :multilocus, erosion = true) converges to and the kernel's geno_var estimates.

The Gaussian assumption fails when few mutations of large effect hold the variance at a locus (α² = V_m/(2Lμ) large against the locus's share V/(2L), the house-of-cards regime, house_of_cards_vg); there the kernel holds less variance than either, and rare_alleles_vg fits it.

QTLSim.gaussian_vg — Function
gaussian_vg(p)

Lande's (1975) weak-mutation form of the equilibrium genetic variance of a Gaussian population of one haploid locus (or of the aggregate recursion), √(V_m V_s), with V_m = m·V_E (muteVar) and V_s from selection_variance. QTLSim.gaussian_vg_exact — Function
gaussian_vg_exact(p)

The fixed point of the aggregate Gaussian recursion V ← V − V²/(V_s + V) + V_m, (V_m + √(V_m² + 4 V_m V_s))/2, which run_moments(p; loci = :aggregate) iterates. It is √(V_m V_s)·(1 + O(√(V_m/V_s))), close to gaussian_vg when mutation is weak relative to selection (Lean gaussian_fixed_point). For a diploid of L recombining loci the equilibrium is gaussian_vg_loci, the same formula with 2L·V_m for V_m (10× larger at 50 loci in the paper's regime, 7.6× with drift at N = 1000).

QTLSim.house_of_cards_vg — Function
house_of_cards_vg(p; nsurv = p.PopSize)

Bürger's stochastic house-of-cards approximation to the equilibrium genetic variance, 4nμ V_s / (1 + V_s/(N_e α²)), with n loci, mutation rate μ per locus per gamete, α² = V_m/(2nμ) the variance of one mutation's effect, and N_e from effective_size with nsurv survivors per generation (Bürger 1989; the paper's Supplementary Figure S1). QTLSim.house_of_gauss_vg — Function
house_of_gauss_vg(p; nsurv = p.PopSize, drift = true)

The equilibrium genetic variance of L loci in linkage equilibrium under mutation, stabilising selection and drift, interpolating the neutral, house-of-cards and Gaussian regimes without a fitted constant: Hermisson and Wagner's (2004, equation A4) "stochastic house of Gauss", V_G = Ploidy·L·c with each gene's variance c the positive root of

c²/V_s + c (α²/(2V_s) + 1/(Ploidy·N_e)) = μα²,

i.e. c = 2μα²/(b + √(b² + 4μα²/V_s)), b = α²/(2V_s) + 1/(Ploidy·N_e), with μ = MutationRatePerGamete, α² = QTLSim.mutation_effect_variance(p), V_s = selection_variance(p) and N_e from effective_size with nsurv survivors (N_e = ∞ with drift = false). A haploid genome, inherited whole by its clones (or a diploid's haplotype when no pair of loci recombines), is one unit with Lμ in place of μ and V_G = Ploidy·c (src/rareallelestraits.jl). Its limits are Lynch and Hill's neutral 2N_e V_m (V_s → ∞), Bürger's stochastic house of cards house_of_cards_vg (rare mutations, μ → 0), Turelli's house of cards 4Lμ V_s (rare mutations, no drift) and the Gaussian √(2L V_m V_s) (many mutations, gaussian_vg_loci's weak-mutation form). It is the fixed point of the per-gene recursion behind the rare-alleles closure (src/rarealleles.jl); [`rareallelesvg](@ref) is the same closure in the multilocus model, with linkage disequilibrium ands = Vs + V_G`.

QTLSim.steady_lag — Function
steady_lag(k; VG, Vs)
steady_lag(p; VG)

The lag θ − z̄ at which a population whose mean responds to Gaussian selection keeps pace with an optimum moving k per generation: k (V_G + V_s)/V_G (Lande; Bürger and Lynch 1995). The response to a lag λ is V_G λ/(V_G + V_s) per generation, which equals k at that lag (Lean steady_lag_response). With p, k = MoveFitnessSD and V_s = selection_variance(p), for MoveType = :continuous.

With phenotypic plasticity, a reaction norm of slope b where the optimum has slope B on the environment (Plasticity and OptimumSlope of p), the plastic response tracks b/B of the optimum's move and the genotype has to follow the rest, at (1 − b/B) k per generation, so the lag is (1 − b/B) k (V_G + V_s)/V_G (after Chevin, Lande and Mace 2010; derived here from the recursion ḡ' = ḡ + β (θ − ḡ − (b/B) θ_{t−τ})). A constant delay τ of the cue (CueLag) does not change it: the genotype makes up the constant shortfall.

QTLSim.critical_rate — Function
critical_rate(; n, VG, ω, VE, sd50, N, b = 0, B = 1, ωb = Inf)

Bürger and Lynch's critical rate of environmental change, as the paper's results table computes it, in units of the phenotypic standard deviation sd50 at 50% mortality:

k_c = V_G/(V_G + V_s) · √(2 V_λ ln B₀),   V_s = ω² + V_E,  V_λ = V_s + V_G + V_s/(2N_e),
B₀ = N₀ ω/√V_λ,   N₀ = N(2n − 3)/(2n(n − 2)),   V_k = 2N₀(1 − 2N₀/N),  N_e = 4N/(V_k + 2),

QTLSim.extinction_distribution — Function
extinction_distribution(p, Wbar; N0 = p.PopSize, from = 0, method = :chain)
extinction_distribution(p, m; from = 0, method = :chain)

The distribution of the time to extinction from demographic stochasticity alone, along a deterministic trajectory of mean fitness: Wbar[t+1] is the mean fitness W̄_t in generation t, or m is the result of run_moments(p) (with Sample = 1), whose mean_fitness it takes. The population starts with N0 adults in generation 0; with from, the stochastic process starts in generation from, with the deterministic size of that generation, and is certain to survive before it.

In generation t each of the N_t adults survives with probability W̄_t (viability selection; the binomial ignores the variation of fitness among individuals), and the population is lost if fewer than two survive (one, if haploid). Under QTSim's Demography = :fixed the survivors are replaced by PopSize offspring, so each generation is an independent trial, lost with probability P(Binomial(PopSize, W̄_t) < 2). Under :beverton_holt the n survivors have min(K, Poisson(R n/(1 + (R − 1) n/K))) offspring (noffspring), and method chooses how this is followed:

  • :branching: the density dependence is linearised along the deterministic trajectory. Each survivor has Poisson(r_t) offspring independently, with r_t = R/(1 + (R − 1) n_t/K) the per-capita rate at the expected number of survivors n_t = N_t W̄_t, so the adults form a Galton–Watson process in a varying environment, with per-adult generating function φ_t(s) = 1 − W̄_t + W̄_t exp(r_t (s − 1)) (survival thins the Poisson). Then P(extinct by T) = (φ_from ∘ ⋯ ∘ φ_T)(0)^{N_from}, by backward iteration. This ignores the cap and the need for two parents, and, near K, the regulation that pulls the size back to the trajectory, so it overstates the spread of sizes and brings extinction forward (vignette 10);
  • :chain (the default): the exact distribution of the size given the trajectory, a Markov chain on 0:K driven by W̄_t, with the binomial survival, the cap at K and the need for two parents. It takes about a second for K = 1000 and 2000 generations.

Returns (; gen, pext, median, psurvive): the generations from:T−1, P(extinct by the end of generation t) for each, the median generation of extinction (missing if pext stays below one half), and the probability of surviving the whole trajectory. A trajectory m that run_moments ends with a deterministic extinction in generation m.extinct_gen is certain to be extinct by then (the last gen, with pext = 1).

QTLSim.periodic_response — Function
periodic_response(P; VG, Vs)

The steady response of the mean phenotype to an optimum that cycles with period P generations, θ_t = A sin(2π t/P), when the mean follows z̄_{t+1} = z̄_t + β (θ_t − z̄_t) with β = V_G/(V_G + V_s). With ν = 2π/P, the mean cycles as z̄_t = gain·A sin(ν t − phase), where

D = √(1 − 2(1 − β) cos ν + (1 − β)²),   gain = β/D,

QTLSim.red_noise_lag_var — Function
red_noise_lag_var(σ, ρ; VG, Vs, b = 0, B = 1, τ = 0)

The stationary variance of the lag θ − z̄ when the optimum is AR(1) noise with standard deviation σ and autocorrelation ρ, θ_t = ρ θ_{t−1} + σ√(1 − ρ²) ξ_t, and the mean follows z̄_{t+1} = z̄_t + β (θ_t − z̄_t) with β = V_G/(V_G + V_s):

E[(θ − z̄)²] = 2σ² (1 − ρ) / ((2 − β)(1 − ρ (1 − β))).

White noise (ρ = 0) gives 2σ²/(2 − β), more than σ², as the mean chases noise that has already gone; slowly wandering noise (ρ → 1) is tracked and the lag vanishes.

A perfect cue (τ = 0) gives (1 − φ)² times the variance without plasticity; an old cue of a fast-changing environment (ρ^τ → 0) makes plasticity add noise, (1 + φ²) times it.

QTLSim.sustainable_rate — Function
sustainable_rate(p; VG, ω = initFitSD(p), b = p.Plasticity)

The largest rate of change of the optimum (per generation, MoveType = :continuous) at which the deterministic recursion of the moments lowering persists, for a population with genetic variance VG: at the steady lag λ = (1 − b/B) k (V_G + V_s)/V_G (steady_lag) the mean fitness is

W̄(λ) = c·exp(−b²/(2ω_b²))·ω/√s·exp(−λ²/(2s)),   s = ω² + V_G + V_E,  c = 1/(0.4√(2π)),

k_max = V_G/s · √(2 s ln(W̄(0)/W_min)) / |1 − b/B|,

QTLSim.migration_mixing — Function
migration_mixing(M, N, alive)

The expected composition of the demes after the kernel's migration (migrate!), with M[a, b] the probability that a visit to an individual of deme a starts an exchange with a random individual of deme b, N the deme sizes and alive which demes take part: P[a, c] is the expected fraction of deme a that came from deme c.

migrate! visits the demes in order, and each individual of a deme once. When deme a is visited, its individuals all have the composition of the deme, P[a, :] (earlier demes chose partners in it at random), and its unvisited ones do not change while it is visited. Each visit exchanges with deme b with probability r = M[a, b], sending an individual of composition P[a, :] and receiving a random one of b, whose expected composition moves toward P[a, :] by a factor 1 − r/N_b per visit. Over the N_a visits, with f = (1 − r/N_b)^{N_a}, deme b becomes P[a, :] + f (P[b, :] − P[a, :]) and deme a receives N_a r P[a, :] + N_b (1 − f)(P[b, :] − P[a, :]), in place of the N_a r individuals it sends. These expectations are exact for any deme sizes, given the order in which the demes are visited.

migrate! draws that order uniformly at random at every call (so that migration is exchangeable, Migration.lean), and the expected composition is the average of the above over all k! orders: exactly, by enumerating them, for k ≤ 8, and over 20,000 random orders from a fixed seed (so deterministically, to about 1%) for more demes.

QTLSim.shared_blocks — Function
shared_blocks(w, i, c, v, j, d) -> BitVector

For each block position, whether chromosome c of individual i of w and chromosome d of individual j of v (current adults of populations that share a block pool, as the demes of a metapopulation made by ⊗ do; v may be w) hold the same block.

What block identity means

A block id is given to a chromosome when the block is made: at the start (one block of zeros per position, shared by everyone, unless tag_founders! or setgenomes! gave each chromosome its own), by a crossover strictly inside the block position between two different blocks (the recombinant is a new block), or by a mutation in a block that another slot also holds (copy on write; a block held by one slot only is changed in place and keeps its id, which nobody else can then hold). Every other transmission copies the id. Therefore, for two chromosomes present at the same time:

  • The same id implies that both blocks are copies, through whole-block transmissions only, of one block of one ancestral chromosome X (the one in which the block was made, or last changed in place): at every locus of the block the two chromosomes are identical by descent from X, their most recent common ancestor at the locus is X or a descendant of it, no mutation has occurred anywhere in the block on either lineage below that ancestor, and the alleles are identical in state. With the initial shared blocks of zeros, X is the founding block itself, not a chromosome: then the same id says only that neither lineage has mutated or been recut in the block since the start (use tag_founders! to make ids count descent from individual founders).
  • Different ids imply nothing. The two may still be identical by descent at some or all loci of the block: one lineage may carry a mutation elsewhere in the block, or the block may have been rebuilt by a crossover inside it (the loci on each side remain copies of their sources), or the genomes came through setgenomes! or through migration between pools.

So block identity is a lower bound on identity by descent: recent, unbroken and unmutated. It is exact (same id if and only if identical by descent without an intervening mutation at the locus) when blocks are single loci (World(p; blocksize = 1)) and founders are tagged, since no crossover falls inside a block of one locus.

Ids are recycled when a block is freed, so ids saved from an earlier generation mean nothing later; compare only chromosomes that are alive together. Ids of different pools are unrelated (an ArgumentError).

Nei's (1973) definition, the identity analogue of Slatkin's (1991) (T̄ − T̄₀)/T̄ for coalescence times. pairwise[a, b] is the same for the two demes a and b alone.

From a metapopulation ws, the identities are those of block ids (ibd_sharing): identity by descent without an intervening mutation or recut in the block, which is identity in state under an infinite-alleles model of blocks. The identities of a pedigree (pair_coalescence, coalescence_cdf) can be passed as within and between in the same way.

A pedigree records, for every offspring chromosome, the parental chromosomes it copies and the crossovers between them, with the mutations and the migrations, as tree-sequence tables. From it come the complete ancestry of every locus and the realised coalescence times that deme_coalescence predicts.

QTLSim.record_pedigree! — Function
record_pedigree!(w::World; simplify_every = 0, mutations = true, tag = true) -> Pedigree
record_pedigree!(ws::AbstractVector{World}; ...)

Start recording the pedigree of a population, or of the demes ws of a metapopulation (for a metapopulation s made by ⊗, record_pedigree!(s)), from the current adults, the founders, on. Returns the Pedigree, which the populations fill as they run (run!, generation!, run_composed, the operators): for every offspring its parents; for each of its chromosomes the parental chromosomes it copies and where it switches between them (so the ancestor of every locus, whatever happens to block ids); the mutations, with their effects (mutations = true); and each migration between demes.

Recording draws no random numbers and changes no value: a run with a pedigree is the same run, bit for bit, as without one. It is off by default, and a World without a pedigree does no work for it (the kernel still allocates nothing per generation).

Cost

Without simplification the tables grow by every individual of every generation: 8 bytes per chromosome, 20 per individual, 16 per edge (one per gamete, two with a crossover; more under CrossoverModel = :all), 12 + 8·NoTraits per mutation and 16 per migration, about 140 bytes per diploid individual per generation with one crossover and one mutation per gamete; see validate/coalescence.log for measured time and memory. The founders' non-zero alleles are recorded as mutations at time 0, 20 bytes each, so starting from a population that has already evolved costs up to 20·NoQTL bytes per founder chromosome (nothing for a fresh World(p), whose alleles are zero). With simplify_every = n the tables are simplified (simplify!) every n generations, which keeps only what is ancestral to the living population: memory is then bounded by the genealogy of the living chromosomes (at most 2Nk − 1 coalescence nodes per distinct marginal tree) plus n generations of new records, at the price of the individuals in between (their identities, parents and migrations).

tag = true first gives each founder chromosome its own blocks (tag_founders!), so that block identity agrees with the pedigree's founders. Do not write genomes (setgenomes!, lower!) while recording: the pedigree would not know about them.

QTLSim.Pedigree — Type
Pedigree

The recorded ancestry of a population or metapopulation (record_pedigree!), as tables in the style of a tree sequence (Kelleher et al. 2018). Times are generations since recording began: the founders are born at 0, their offspring at 1.

  • Nodes, one per chromosome of every recorded individual: node_time[n], the generation in which it was born, and node_ind[n], its individual. Node ids increase with the time of birth, so a parent's id is below its child's.
  • Individuals: ind_time[i], ind_birth[i] the deme it was born in, ind_deme[i] the deme it was an adult in (after migration), and ind_p1[i], ind_p2[i] its parents (0 for a founder, for a haploid's second parent, and for a parent that simplification removed). An individual's first chromosome came from ind_p1.
  • Edges (left[e], right[e], parent[e], child[e]): node child inherited loci left+1:right (the half-open interval [left, right) of 0-based positions, as in tskit) from node parent. A gamete without a crossover is one edge, one with a crossover after locus x two, [0, x) and [x, L).
  • Mutations (when mutations): mut_node[m], mut_locus[m] (1-based), mut_time[m] and the effect added, mut_effect[m] (with n traits, the n effects at (m-1)n+1:mn), in the order they happened. A founder's non-zero alleles are mutations at time 0, so that an allele is the sum, oldest first, of the mutations on its lineage.
  • Migrations: individual mig_ind[e] moved from deme mig_from[e] to mig_to[e] after the generation mig_time[e] in which it was born, in the order they happened.

worlds are the populations recorded, in deme order, and simplify_every the interval between simplifications (simplify!; 0 for never), the last at last_simplified.

QTLSim.simplify! — Function
simplify!(ped) -> ped

Reduce the pedigree to the genealogy of the living chromosomes (samples): the algorithm of Kelleher et al. (2018, PLoS Comput. Biol. 14: e1006581). It keeps the samples and every node in which two or more lineages ancestral to samples join at some locus, with edges only over the intervals where they do; nodes through which a single lineage passes, ancestry above the most recent common ancestor of all samples at a locus, and everything not ancestral to a sample are dropped. The marginal tree of the samples at every locus, with the times of its nodes, is unchanged, so coalescence times are exact; mutations are moved to the node below them on the same lineage, so allele still rebuilds every living allele bit for bit. Individuals without a remaining node are dropped with their migrations, and a parent that was dropped becomes 0.

record_pedigree!(…; simplify_every = n) calls it every n generations, when every living deme has finished the generation. Called by hand, call it between generations.

The life-cycle morphism f with parameters given to its steps. The life cycle cycle lowered to the moments of a Gaussian population (lower(cycle, :moments)) and iterated for NoGenerations: the predictions of quantitative-genetic theory for the same morphism the kernel and SLiM simulate. Each step's image updates the means and variances:

  • develop: V_P = V_G + V_E;
  • select: moves the optimum as MoveType says, then applies Gaussian selection of width ω to z ~ N(z̄, V_P). This gives the mean fitness, the expected survivors N·W̄, the selection differential, and the survivors' genotypic mean and variance z̄ + V_G λ/s and V_G − V_G²/s, with λ = θ − z̄ and s = ω² + V_P;
  • reproduce: the offspring inherit the survivors' mean; their variance is what recombination makes of the survivors' (below);
  • mutate: adds V_m = m·V_E.

Which model (loci). The default, loci = :auto, is the rare-alleles closure (:rare_alleles, below) for one trait in one population (diploid, or haploid clones), the closure with locally favoured alleles (:rare_local, below; :rare_alleles for one deme) for several traits and for one trait in several demes (compared with the kernel in validate/rare_alleles_traits.jl and validate/local_adaptation.jl), and the multilocus Gaussian model (:multilocus) when the mutate steps of cycle carry different MutationScalar or MutationRatePerGamete (no one mutation effect variance); QTLSim.moments_loci gives the resolution for a Params. Ask for loci = :multilocus, :rare_alleles, :rare_local or :aggregate to use those models anywhere (the closure for several traits and for haploid clones is in src/rareallelestraits.jl). The rare-alleles closure is within 15% of the kernel in 46 of 47 weak-selection cells of validate/moments_regimes.jl, never farther from it than the multilocus Gaussian, and up to 10× closer in the house-of-cards and drift regimes; when mutations are many and small the two agree.

The multilocus model (loci = :multilocus). The population is NoQTL additive loci whose allelic values are jointly Gaussian (Lande 1975; Bulmer 1980; Turelli and Barton 1990). The lowering carries the expected second moments of the haplotypes (one gamete's alleles summed over the loci): the variance T of one haplotype, the covariance R of an adult's two and the covariance Q of haplotypes of two distinct adults, each in full and restricted to alleles at one locus (the genic parts; the difference is linkage disequilibrium, LD). Selection lowers a haplotype's variance, and the covariance of an adult's two, by D = (V_G/2)²/s. Each locus carries 1/NoQTL of the variance, so the genic parts fall by D/NoQTL, and the rest is negative LD. At reproduction an offspring's two haplotypes come from distinct parents (R ← Q). Recombination turns LD back into variance, the LD of each class of pairs of loci at its own rate (QTLSim.pair_classes, from the kernel's crossover; their mean is pair_recombination). Mutation adds V_m/2 to each haplotype. A haploid's offspring is a clone of one parent. The equations are in src/multilocus.jl (QTLSim.hap_select, hap_reproduce, hap_mutate). Without drift (the default) the population is infinite, and one deme converges to gaussian_vg_loci, V²/(V_s + V) = 2L·V_m for a diploid of L loci that recombine. With erosion = true the deme has its expected N adults and n parents (the expected survivors, or under fecundity selection N W̄²/E[W²]), and drift enters through Q: two offspring share a parent with probability 1/n. Then geno_var is the expected within-population sample variance (divisor N − 1, as the kernel records it), converging to gaussian_vg_loci(p; drift = true). Under a continuous move the lag θ − z̄ converges to steady_lag with that variance. The model is good when many small mutations hold each locus's variance; when few large ones do (the house-of-cards regime, house_of_cards_vg) the kernel holds less (validate/moments_loci.jl).

The rare-alleles closure (loci = :rare_alleles; the default for one trait). The multilocus model with the fourth cumulant of a locus's alleles set to α² times their variance (α² = QTLSim.mutation_effect_variance(p), the variance of one mutation's effect): selection also removes β²α²/(2s) of the genic variance, each rare mutant at its own rate, with β = H/H_genic the regression of the genotype on a locus's alleles (β = 1 without LD). It equals the multilocus Gaussian when mutations are many and small, and reduces to the (stochastic) house of cards when they are rare and large and to 2N V_m when selection is weak against drift; its equilibrium is rare_alleles_vg, and for loci in linkage equilibrium house_of_gauss_vg. Equations in src/rarealleles.jl; the kernel comparison across regimes, including the paper's populations, is `validate/momentsregimes.jl. With drift (erosion = true) one deme converges torareallelesvg(p; drift = true), and under a continuous move the lag to [steady_lag](@ref) with that variance. Each deme of a metapopulation has its ownα²; when themutatesteps ofcyclecarryMutationScalarorMutationRatePerGamete(all the same),α²` is computed with them.

With several traits (loci = :rare_alleles) a locus's genic covariance c also loses ½(𝓡 c + c 𝓡ᵀ), with 𝓡 = ½ C X⁻¹, C the covariance of one mutation's effect vector and X = E[a aᵀ/(aᵀ s̃⁻¹ a)] (s̃⁻¹ = βᵀ s⁻¹ β): exact at the multivariate house-of-cards balance, α²/(2s) for one trait (QTLSim.rare_alleles_rate). Its equilibrium is rare_alleles_G, and for loci in linkage equilibrium house_of_gauss_G, the stabilising solution of an algebraic Riccati equation. Haploid offspring are clones, so a haploid genome is one unit of inheritance: the closure then acts on a haplotype's whole variance (β = 1, QTLSim.hap_clonal), with L μ mutations per genome.

Locally favoured alleles (loci = :rare_local). The rare-alleles closure for metapopulations whose demes lag behind different optima (src/localalleles.jl). A rare mutant of effect a has selection coefficient `aᵀℓd − ½aᵀsd⁻¹ain demed,ℓd = sd⁻¹(θd − z̄d); under migration (backward matrixP) its lineage grows atπ·σ + σᵀWσto second order,W = Π(I − P)^#P, so the variation of the lags among the demes weakens selection against it by2Λ,Λ = Σ Wdc (ℓd − ℓ̄)(ℓc − ℓ̄)ᵀ([QTLSim.localgain](@ref)), the island model's(1 − m′)/m′·Var(ℓ). The closure's rare loss uses[s⁻¹ − 2Λ]₊([QTLSim.localsinv](@ref)) in place ofs⁻¹; nothing else changes. The allele-frequency spectrum (loci = :spectrum, one diploid trait, one deme). A model of how the variance changes under a moving optimum (src/spectrum.jl): instead of haplotype moments, the expected numbers of segregating mutations by effect and frequency (Sawyer and Hartl's Poisson random field, infinite sites), each moved by E[Δp] = pq[aλ/S − (1 − λ²/S)(a² + 2c)(1 − 2p)/(2S)] (Hayward and Sella 2022, eq. 7, with the rare-alleles closure's Gaussian term 2c, c = V_G/(2L)) and drift among the parents, which linked selection under directional selection lowers by N/N_e = 1 + b²V_G Σ_c ω_c/(2(ρ_c + δ)²) (Barton 1995; b = λ/S, the crossover's pair classes, δ = 1/(2n) + V_G/S); 2NLμ mutations of effect N(0, α²) enter at frequency 1/(2N) each generation. V_G = Σ 2a²pq, and selection moves the mean by Σ 2a E[Δp], which includes the alleles' skew. Drift is always on (erosion does not matter); a population started with VG > 0 takes the stationary spectrum's shape. At a fixed optimum it converges to spectrum_vg, close to house_of_gauss_vg for rare mutations; it is meant for 4Nμ ≲ 1 per locus (in the Gaussian regime, many mutations per locus, its VG is too high). Under a move it captures both the rise of VG when rare alleles of large effect sweep (house of cards; Barton and Turelli 1987) and its collapse at slow rates in small or declining populations (fast turnover by sweeps, falling Ne, linked selection); `validate/vgmove.jl.spectrumpasses options toQTLSim.AlleleSpectrum:grid(frequencies, 240),classes(effects, 20) andlinkage(:barton, or:none` for linkage equilibrium).

The aggregate recursion (loci = :aggregate). QTLSim's moments lowering before the multilocus model: the offspring inherit the survivors' variance, V ← V − V²/(V_s + V) + V_m, converging to gaussian_vg_exact. It treats all of selection's reduction as inherited, which is exact for one haploid locus (and for haploid clones), but not for diploids: for one diploid locus half of it (between the haplotypes) is lost at the next reproduction, and for many loci most of it is LD that recombination breaks up. With erosion = true drift multiplies the offspring's variance by 1 − 1/(2n).

The expected population size is PopSize, or under Demography = :beverton_holt the mean of noffspring for the expected survivors, R n/(1 + (R − 1) n/K).

Returns the columns of run!, predicted (nsurv rounded). The population is extinct when fewer than two individuals (one, if haploid) are expected to survive; extinction_distribution adds the demographic stochasticity along the trajectory. Requires Catlab.

The fluctuating moves lower to their expected optimum (fluctuating_optimum without noise): MoveType = :periodic exactly, since it is deterministic, and :red_noise as its linear trend. Several demes. QTLSim.migrate! — Function
migrate!(s, rng; rate = 0.01)

Move individuals between the demes of a metapopulation s: each individual starts a swap with probability rate, exchanging places with a random individual of a random other deme, so the deme sizes are unchanged. Returns (; count), the number of exchanges. Requires Catlab.

rate is not the island model's migration rate m: an individual also moves when another deme's swap picks it, so with equal deme sizes about 2·rate of each deme is replaced per generation (each exchange moves two individuals). Use m/2 for an island-model rate m. The SLiM lowering (migRate) does the same.

When rate is a matrix M (island_migration, stepping_stone), an individual of the deme in position a starts an exchange with probability M[a, b] with a random individual of the deme in position b: migration along a graph. One uniform draw decides whether and where, so a matrix draws differently from a number, even island_migration(k, r), which is the same model as rate = r. Demes must have the same loci and ploidy, and may differ in size.

The swaps are made one after the other, so an individual that has just arrived in a deme can move again when that deme takes its turn. The order of the turns therefore matters: with the demes always visited in the order 1, …, k, migration would not commute with relabelling the demes, not even for the island model (k = 2 demes of 2 individuals are a counterexample). Each call therefore visits the demes in a uniformly random order (a randperm(rng, k)), and the individuals of each deme in order. Then migration commutes with every relabelling that preserves the matrix, in distribution: every relabelling for the island model (island_migrate_after_generation_equivariant in lean/CategoricalQuantGen/Migration.lean), the rotations and reflections of the ring for stepping_stone. Extinct demes are skipped. The SLiM lowering draws its order the same way.

A statistic σ maps one backend's states to another's; agreement between backends is a naturality square σ ∘ K(f) = M(f) ∘ σ, exact for some life cycles and within a closure's error for others. NaturalityCase(; name, source = "", cycle = nothing, params, from = :kernel, to = :moments,

A naturality square as data: one row of the table of validation cases (validate/naturality_cases.jl). cycle is the life-cycle morphism (nothing: QTSim's generation; for k demes a morphism on Zygotes^⊗k), params a Params or one per deme, from and to two of the backends :kernel, :slim (stochastic: replicated), :moments and :theory (deterministic: run once). statistics is a vector of statistic => tolerance pairs (QTLSim.Statistic), and criterion how each is compared, with its tolerance written before the run:

QTLSimCatlabExt — Module
QTLSimCatlabExt

The categorical layer of QTLSim, loaded with Catlab. The life cycle is a morphism of a free category whose objects are the stages of a generation (lifecycle.jl), lowered by functors to the simulation kernel, where each step is an Operator composed with Catlab's ⋅ and stratified into demes with its monoidal product ⊗ (compose.jl), and to a SLiM model in Eidos (eidos.jl), and to the deterministic recursions of quantitative-genetic theory on a Gaussian population's moments (moments.jl). The population is an attributed C-set (ACSet) on the schema of schema.jl. The facts that justify the decomposition, that composition is associative, that a run is the iterate of one composed generation and that a lowering is fixed by its action on the steps, are proved in lean/CategoricalQuantGen/Composition.lean and Lowering.lean.

QTLSimCatlabExt.with_step_params — Function
with_step_params(model, s::Step, op::Operator)
