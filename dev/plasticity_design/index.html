Phenotypic plasticity: a reaction norm through the same life cycle

Plasticity is the standard reason a population might keep up with an optimum that moves faster than its genes can follow. This note records how a linear reaction norm (Chevin, Lande and Mace 2010; Lande 2009) went through QTLSim's life cycle and its three lowerings, what it is in the terms of Several traits, and what is not supported.

The model

The environment ε is measured by the optimum it sets: θ = B·ε, with B (OptimumSlope, 1 by default) the environmental sensitivity of selection. An individual's phenotype is

z = a + b·ε_cue + e,      ε_cue = θ_{t−τ}/B,      e ~ N(0, V_E),

with elevation a (its breeding value in the reference environment ε = 0, where the optimum is the origin of the trait's scale), slope b, and a cue that is the environment τ = CueLag generations before selection. τ = 0 is a perfect cue, development and selection in the same environment. The environment before the first generation is taken to have been the first one seen. A cost of plasticity is stabilising selection on the slope around 0: fitness is multiplied by exp(−b²/(2ω_b²)), ω_b = PlasticityCostSD (Inf by default: no cost).

Only the ratio b/B matters for a fixed slope: the plastic part of the phenotype is (b/B)·θ_{t−τ}, the fraction of the optimum's displacement that is tracked without any genetic change. B is kept as a parameter so that slopes and costs can be given in the units of the literature.

Two models:

  • A fixed slope (Plasticity = b, one trait). Every individual has the same slope, so the plastic part is common to all and a = g, QTSim's genotype. This is a parameter of one step, not a new model.
  • A heritable reaction norm (ReactionNorm = true, NoTraits = 2). The two heritable values of the model of several traits are the elevation, a = g₁, and the deviation of the slope from Plasticity, b = Plasticity + g₂. Mutation is the pleiotropic mutation of two traits (MutCov is the mutational covariance of elevation and slope). Selection acts on the one phenotype z, with width ω, and the cost on the individual's slope.

Everything is off by default: with Plasticity = 0 and ReactionNorm = false the kernel's output is bit for bit what it was, a generation allocates nothing, and slim_script writes the same file (the golden-file test).

Where plasticity sits in the life cycle

QTSim's generation is develop ⋅ select ⋅ reproduce ⋅ record ⋅ rescale ⋅ mutate ⋅ swap, and the optimum of generation t is set in select, after develop has formed the phenotypes (QTSim's PopFitness: phenotypes, then SetFitnessMean, then fitness). The environment a perfect cue reports is therefore not known when develop runs. So:

  • develop is unchanged: it gives the phenotype in the reference environment, g + e.
  • select moves the optimum, then expresses the norm, then computes fitness. The plasticity fields (Plasticity, OptimumSlope, CueLag, PlasticityCostSD) are parameters of select (step_schema(OneTraitModel())[:select]), so withparams(L.select; Plasticity = 0.5) is a step with its own slope, and demes can have their own. record then records the expressed phenotypes.

The alternative, moving the optimum in develop, would keep "development" in develop but would change which step owns the move (the move's fields are select's, a select step can carry its own move, and QTSim's :phenotype and :fitness moves need the phenotypes). It was not done. A lagged cue (τ ≥ 1) could have been expressed in develop; it is expressed in select too, so that there is one place.

No new generator was needed, and for a fixed slope no new object image either: the syntax, the stages and the ACSet schema are unchanged.

Is the heritable norm a third model?

Yes, a small one: ReactionNormModel() (model_of(p) when ReactionNorm is set). It is best described against TraitModel(2):

TraitModel(2)ReactionNormModel()
object image (an individual)g, e, z ∈ ℝ²g ∈ ℝ², e, z ∈ ℝ
developz = g + ethe same image (the pair's sums); z is formed in select
selectW = c·exp(−½ (z−θ)ᵀΩ⁻¹(z−θ)), θ ∈ ℝ²z = (1, ε)·(a, b) + e, W = c·exp(−(z−θ)²/2ω²)·exp(−b²/2ω_b²), θ ∈ ℝ

recordtrait means, G, Pthe same code; the "traits" recorded are (z, b)

So it is TraitModel(2) with a linear map before selection: the pair (a, b) is read through (a, b) ↦ a + b·ε, a map that changes every generation with the environment, and selection is one-dimensional on the image. It is not TraitModel(2) with a clever Ω: the fitness function on the pair, exp(−½ (g − g*)ᵀ Q (g − g*)) with Q = v vᵀ/(ω² + V_E) + e₂e₂ᵀ/ω_b², v = (1, ε), is singular without a cost (rank one), has no fixed optimum and changes with ε; and the environmental deviation is one number, not two. But all the genetics is shared: the block pool with two values per locus, recombination, pleiotropic mutation, migration. In the kernel the model's table is TraitModel(2)'s with select replaced by QTLSim.select_norm!; in the moments lowering trait_moment_images with select and record replaced (the haplotype moments of the multilocus model take selection through the matrix K with G_s = G − G K G, where TraitModel uses (Ω + P)⁻¹). Its step parameters are typed as one trait's for select (ω and θ numbers, the plasticity fields) and as two traits' for mutate (MutCorr, MutCov).

In the language of the north star: the object image did not change from TraitModel(2)'s for the heritable state, and the new ingredient is a morphism of the environment, ℝ² × Env → ℝ, composed before the image of select. A general framework would want that map as a first-class part of a model (a genotype–phenotype map parametrised by the environment), of which QTSim's identity and this linear map are instances.

The Eidos splice follows the pattern of the fluctuating moves and of fecundity selection: the default output is byte-identical, and validate/slim/compare_plasticity.jl compares the kernel with the generated SLiM model.

Theory

With a fixed slope and φ = b/B the mean genotype follows ḡ′ = ḡ + β (θ_t − ḡ − φ θ_{t−τ}), β = V_G/(V_G + V_s): the recursion without plasticity for the effective optimum θ_t − φ θ_{t−τ}. All of the following are derived from that recursion (and checked symbolically in validate/symbolic/check_plasticity.jl), not transcribed from the literature; the equations of Chevin, Lande and Mace (2010) are images in the published article and could not be read by the tools used here, so only their statements in the text were checked against the source (that the critical rate increases as |B − b| falls, that a cost lowers the maximum growth rate, and that above a threshold cost an intermediate plasticity maximises the critical rate).

  • An exact correspondence. With a perfect cue (τ = 0) and no cost, z − θ = g + e − (1 − φ)θ: fitness depends on the genotype and the optimum only through (1 − φ)θ. So a plastic population whose optimum moves k per generation is, in distribution and not only at steady state, a population without plasticity whose optimum moves (1 − φ)k: every statistic of the genotypes, the survivors and the time to extinction is that of the slower move (tested in the kernel: the same survivors and genetic variances for the same seed). The scaling of the sustainable rate by 1/(1 − φ) is therefore exact in this model, whatever the genetics, and the experiment of validate/plasticity_rates.jl confirms it as a check of the implementation. What can depart from it is a cost, a delayed cue of a fluctuating optimum, and a heritable slope.
  • Steady lag behind an optimum moving k per generation: (1 − φ) k (V_G + V_s)/V_G (steady_lag), whatever the cue's delay: the genotype has to supply only (1 − φ) k per generation.
  • Critical rate: critical_rate (Bürger and Lynch's, in the paper's table form) divided by |1 − φ|, with ln B₀ reduced by the cost b²/(2ω_b²). sustainable_rate is the same threshold for the package's own demography: the rate at which the steady lag brings the mean fitness to 1/R (Beverton–Holt) or to Ploidy/N (QTSim's fixed size). With a cost an intermediate slope can be best.
  • Fluctuating optima: the lag is L_t − φ L_{t−τ} with L the lag without plasticity. For red noise its variance is (1 + φ²)Γ(0) − 2φΓ(τ) (red_noise_lag_var, red_noise_lag_autocov): a perfect cue scales the variance by (1 − φ)², an old cue of a fast environment makes plasticity add noise. For a cycle the squared amplitude is multiplied by 1 + φ² − 2φ cos(2πτ/P) (expected_lag2). The cost adds b²/(2ω_b²) to lag_load.
  • A heritable norm (reaction_norm_response, reaction_norm_lag): without a cost Δ(ā, b̄) = G v λ/s with s = ω² + V_E + vᵀGv. The slope responds in proportion to ε, so plasticity evolves only away from the reference environment; there the slope's variance inflates the phenotypic variance by G_bb ε² (a variance load) and is used up by selection. Under a sustained move the mean slope approaches B as d b̄/dt ≈ (B − b̄)/t, and the lag falls with it: with heritable, cost-free plasticity there is no critical rate in this model, only a transient. With a cost, in a constant environment the only equilibrium has b̄ = 0 and the elevation at the optimum (Lande's genetic assimilation); under a sustained move the slope settles below B.

What is rejected, and what is not covered

  • MoveType = :phenotype and :fitness with plasticity: QTSim re-anchors the optimum to the current mean phenotype every generation (θ = z̄ + lead), so a plastic shift of the mean is carried along by the optimum and has no meaning. checkparams rejects them (unless the optimum never moves). Plasticity needs an optimum with its own trajectory: :continuous, :red_noise, :periodic, a step's fixed θ, or none.
  • Plasticity ≠ 0 with NoTraits > 1 needs ReactionNorm; ReactionNorm needs NoTraits = 2, the default optimum direction and no SelCorr/SelCov.
  • The heritable norm has no Eidos lowering (slim_script says so), no rare-alleles closure in the moments (the fitness function on the pair is singular without a cost, so the closure's rate is undefined; loci is :multilocus or :aggregate), and its ACSet is that of two traits.
  • With red noise the moments lowering follows the trend only, as without plasticity; the lag's variance is red_noise_lag_var's.
  • StandardisePhenotype, fecundity selection, density regulation, demes and migration all work with a fixed slope (the shift is common to a deme; a migrant expresses the norm in the deme it develops in). They are tested lightly, not validated separately.
  • The measure of the environment is the optimum itself. An environmental variable that moves the optimum nonlinearly, or cues that are noisy for reasons other than their age, are not modelled.

Validation

  • test/runtests.jl: the default path bit for bit (a slope with the optimum at the origin adds exactly zero), no allocation, the kernel's expression of the norm, reductions at b = 0 (===), kernel against moments and theory, steps and demes with their own slopes, the Eidos splice, and the heritable norm against Monte Carlo and the moments.
  • validate/symbolic/check_plasticity.jl: the algebra above.
  • validate/slim/compare_plasticity.jl: kernel against the generated SLiM model.
  • validate/plasticity_rates.jl: the pre-specified experiment on sustainable rates (plasticity_rates.log, 12,320 kernel runs: two published-like parameter sets with 100 and 500 loci, fixed size and Beverton–Holt R = 2, b/B = 0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, with and without a cost ωb = 0.5, 40 burn-ins of 10,000 generations per cell, censored 2000 generations after the move). Without a cost the half-survival rate rose by 1.33–1.35, 1.98–2.03 and 3.99–4.07 times (95% bootstrap intervals within 1.32–1.38, 1.95–2.07 and 3.93–4.13), against the predicted 1.33, 2 and 4: all 12 cells pass the 15% criterion, as they must, since the correspondence above is exact. With the cost the gain shrinks: at fixed size 1.24–1.26, 1.46–1.55 and 2.11–2.13 times; under Beverton–Holt 1.03–1.06 times at b/B = 0.25, below 0.63 (every run died on the grid) at 0.5, and extinction before the move at 0.75. The deterministic prediction of the cost's effect failed 4 of 12 cells. The cost also lowers VG before the move (more drift among fewer survivors, and under Beverton–Holt a population of about 340), but that is not why: in validate/plasticity_erosion.jl (pre-specified, fresh seeds) the pre-move loss accounts for 3–9% of the cost's effect on k50; the cost acts after the move, 2–7 times more strongly than sustainable_rate's fitness-margin term predicts. sustainable_rate therefore mispredicts a cost of plasticity (open). See the script's header.
  • validate/reaction_norm.jl (reaction_norm.log): the heritable norm, kernel against the moments lowering, 40 runs; 28 of 30 criteria pass. The mean slope (0.2 → 0.96 without a cost, → 0.56 with ωb = 1) is within 0.07 of the moments at every time, the lag within 0.01 phenotypic SD, Gbb within a factor 1.5. The two failures are the quasi-steady lag formula late in the move (1000 and 1500 generations), where the lag has fallen to 0.003 and below, and the formula's ratio is noise.

For Lean

Claims the Julia code relies on that should get Lean lemmas (none is in lean/ yet):

  1. Steady lag with plasticity: for ḡ′ = ḡ + β (θ_t − ḡ − φ θ_{t−τ}) and θ_t = k t, the lag converges to (1 − φ) k/β for every τ (from FluctuatingOptimum.trend_follows by superposition: the effective optimum is a trend of slope (1 − φ) k plus a constant).
  2. Critical rate: W̄(λ) > W_min iff |k| < β √(2 s ln(W̄(0)/W_min))/|1 − φ| at the steady lag, and its reduction to the rate without plasticity at φ = 0.
  3. Autocovariance of the lag behind AR(1) noise: Γ(h + 1) = a Γ(h) − (1 − ρ) ρ^h c₀, its closed form, and Var(L_t − φ L_{t−τ}) = (1 + φ²)Γ(0) − 2φΓ(τ) (extends red_noise_lag_variance).
  4. A cycle with a delayed cue: the amplitude factor |1 − φ e^{−iντ}|.
  5. Selection on a reaction norm: (I + GQ)⁻¹G = G − G K G with K = Q (I + GQ)⁻¹, and for rank-one Q (no cost) the Sherman–Morrison form G − G v vᵀ G/s (a case of Traits.posterior_cov when Q is invertible; the singular case is new).
  6. Equilibria of the norm in a constant environment: with a cost, Δ = 0 iff λ = 0 and b̄ = 0; without, iff λ = 0.
  7. The expressed phenotype is exact when the optimum has not moved: (b/B)·0 = 0, so the run with a slope equals the run without one before the move (the bitwise test).
  8. The exact correspondence: with τ = 0 and no cost the life cycle's kernel with slope b and optimum sequence θ_t equals, as a Markov kernel on genotypes (in Lowering.lean's Kleisli category of PMF), the kernel without plasticity and optimum (1 − b/B)θ_t: the two select images agree on every population, so by stochastic_lowerings_agree the generations do. This is the statement behind "rates scale by 1/(1 − b/B)".
